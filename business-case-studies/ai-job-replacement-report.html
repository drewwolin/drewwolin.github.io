The "AI Layoff" Myth

AI isn't sitting at a desk doing your job. The current wave of tech layoffs is a massive financial gamble: a reallocation of budgets from human headcount (OPEX) to artificial intelligence infrastructure (CAPEX).

💰The Great Budget Reallocation

This section illustrates the core financial trade-off. Companies are intentionally reducing Operational Expenditures (OPEX)—salaries, recruiting, and office space—to fund the Capital Expenditures (CAPEX) required for the AI arms race. This isn't "AI doing the work," it's "investors demanding AI spending and forcing budget cuts to pay for it."

📈The Productivity Disconnect

The theory that AI is replacing jobs falls apart when we look at output. If AI were replacing humans, we would see a massive spike in productivity (more output per worker). Instead, productivity gains are currently flat. This confirms that AI is still a tool in development, not a worker in production.

The "TBD" Risk: The current CAPEX boom is speculative. If productivity doesn't rise significantly by 2026, the high costs of AI electricity and hardware will become unsustainable, likely swinging the pendulum back toward human-led efficiency.

🤝Task Augmentation Theory

Economists argue that AI doesn't delete jobs; it shreds tasks. Every "job" is a bundle of tasks. AI takes the repetitive slices, forcing the human to shift toward higher-value oversight, creative direction, and social intelligence.

A Temporary Accounting Re-weighting

The "AI Layoff" is a misnomer. It is a strategic reallocation of limited corporate capital. Once the infrastructure is built and the 'low hanging fruit' tasks are automated, the need for human creative and strategic headcount will likely rebound to manage the increased output volume.
